# See the Calculus — `seeing-calculus`

**Nine interactive instruments that build, in order, the visual foundation of calculus.** Each one shows *one* thing, opens with two clicks in the browser, and works **offline**: they are `canvas` and arithmetic, **without third-party libraries, without network, without account, and without server**. Nothing leaves your machine — there's nothing to leave, because there's nowhere to go.

The guarantee that matters: they do not illustrate what a text has already  
said, they allow the assertion to be **verified with the ruler**. When  
`o-encontro` says that the derivative is the tangent of the angle, it draws  
the circle and the curve **at the same scale and on the same line of zero** —  
and then the two lengths that the equality compares *are* two lengths equal in  
pixels. Measured: 113 and 111, and the difference is the disk at the tip of  
one of them.

## Quick Start

No installation. Download and open:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/mateusalkimim/seeing-calculus.git
cd seeing-calculus
xdg-open index.html          # Linux · no Windows, duplo clique no index.html
```

Or use it directly online: <https://mateusalkimim.github.io/seeing-calculus/>.

To check the repository (requires Python 3 and Node, just for that):

```bash
python3 auditar_fatias.py --controle   # the gate, with negative control  
python3 gerar_indice.py                # regenerates the index and navigation of the nine
```

Step-by-step instructions by operating system in [`docs/INSTALLATION.md`](docs/INSTALACAO.md).

## Order, and Why It Is Not Arbitrary

One dimension has only position — there's nowhere to curve. It's the second that  
unlocks form, and that's why **the pair becomes a point** is the root and not just  
another beginning: even an angle needs two dimensions to exist.

```
READING ORDER — one line

pair-to-point → the angle → the triangle → the distance → the circle
              → the unwinding → the rate → the meeting
```

**Dependency and order of reading are not the same thing**, and separating  
them is what makes this line honest. The **rate** does not *depend* on  
trigonometry — slope exists without any angle, and the dependency graph keeps  
it on its own branch. But it **uses the word tangent**, which names two  
objects, and that ambiguity needs to be resolved first. That's why the line  
puts it in seventh.

```
DEPENDENCY — two branches, and only here does the tree exist

                    pair-to-point
              ┌───────────┴───────────┐
           the rate    the angle → the triangle → the distance → the circle
              │                  → the unwinding
              └───────────┬───────────┘
                     the meeting
```

This was not decided by argument: with the rate in second, it was the **only  
slice with declared debt** at the gate — it spent sine, cosine, and π before  
anyone was defined. Descending to seventh, the debt zeroed out, and no slice  
owes anything to those that come after. **The gate saw what the argument did  
not see.**

## The Place in the Larger Cycle

These slices are material from **Hypatia**, the teaching component of the Ítaca system, and were born alongside a series of seminars on computer vision and image geometry. The **norm** that governs them does not reside here: it is the `notation-norm.md` of Hypatia, §0b.7, which declared interactive instrument as educational material and defined what this gate demands. **The instrument went out here; the rule stayed there.**

The closest relative is the  
[math-prerequisite-map](https://github.com/mateusalkimim/math-prerequisite-map),  
from where this repository's template and license choice originated.

## Provenance and Guarantees

- **Nothing from third parties here.** No libraries, no frameworks, no external assets downloaded. Each slice is HTML, CSS, and JavaScript written for it, drawing on `canvas`.  
- **Zero network, by contract and by gate.** The `audit_slices.py` rejects any `@import`, `src`, `<link>`, `url()`, or `fetch` external. A clickable `<a href>` is allowed — a link is something the reader chooses to follow, not something the page fetches on its own.  
- **No telemetry, no cookies, no storage.** The slices do not store or transmit anything.  
- **No client data, no material under NDA, no secrets.** There are no keys, tokens, or machine paths versioned here.

## How This Is Verified

Each slice declares, in a machine-readable manifesto, its order and the symbols it addresses:

```html
<!-- fatia: o-triangulo | ordem: 4 | declara: θ tg sen cos | empresta: — -->
```

The gate charges:

```bash
python3 auditar_fatias.py --controle
```

The **navigation** of each slice is generated in the same order — previous, position in the queue, next — and injected into a marked block. Reordering the slices rewrites the nine at once, and the gate disapproves if the announced position diverges from the manifesto. It goes **inline**, and not in a `nav.js`, because each slice **opens alone**: a loose file sent by email has to keep functioning.

No symbol spent without coming from the slice itself or a **previous** one — inheriting from what comes after is not inheriting, it's assuming. No borrowing without someone to repay it later. No idle statement that would serve only to silence the gate. No network request. And **negative control**: the gate injects defects into a copy and demands they be caught, because zero findings only mean something if it proves it can disprove.

The `index.html` is **generated** from `gerar_indice.py`, which reads the  
order and description of the slices themselves. Editing the generated HTML is  
the bug, not the shortcut.
